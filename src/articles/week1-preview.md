PROVO — Utah has put a result on the board. Now BYU gets to put something next to it.

The Cougars host Wyoming on September 5 at **10 p.m. Eastern**, opening their dynasty season with a clean ledger and the expectations that come with the No. 12 spot in the preseason game poll. Utah, ranked No. 19 on that same board, spends Week 1 on a bye after beating Utah State 36–20.

For Tyler’s Cougars, Saturday is the first chance to turn a promising roster into a recognizable offense. For Matt’s Utes, the assignment is quieter: carry the good parts of the opener forward and fix the plays that let the Aggies hang around.

## BYU: give Bachmeier a game he can build on

Bear Bachmeier and LJ Martin have been the names at the center of BYU’s preseason story. Against Wyoming, we finally get a first look at how the quarterback and running back share the work.

The most encouraging version would begin with Martin giving the offense dependable gains and Bachmeier making sound decisions when the defense changes the picture. The quarterback’s running ability gives BYU another option when a passing play breaks down, but an opener is also a test of patience. A checkdown, a throwaway or a modest gain can keep a promising possession alive.

Three things deserve attention: whether BYU avoids giveaways, whether Martin keeps the offense out of long-yardage situations, and whether Bachmeier can finish drives once the field gets shorter. Those will tell us more about the Cougars’ starting point than one spectacular play.

Our preseason Big 12 ballot put BYU first. That remains an editorial projection; the Cougars enter this game **0–0**, with no dynasty performance yet to support or challenge it. Wyoming supplies the first evidence. Arizona visits in Week 2, so BYU also has an immediate reason to leave the opener with a repeatable approach.

**Wasatch Wire pick: BYU 31, Wyoming 17.** That is a preseason judgment, not a claim about Wyoming’s unseen results or tendencies. We expect Martin’s workload and Bachmeier’s ability to threaten in two ways to give the Cougars enough answers at home. The telling detail will be how much of the offense feels dependable by the fourth quarter.

## Utah: keep the balance, cut the explosives

Steve Smith Jr. has a win to work from, and Devon Dampier gave him a strong starting point. Dampier completed 27 of 31 passes for 291 yards against Utah State, added 53 rushing yards and accounted for two touchdowns. Wayshawn Parker supplied 114 yards on 19 carries. Utah finished with 474 yards and no turnovers.

That balance is worth preserving. Parker can carry a meaningful share of the offense, Dampier can move it through the air or on the ground, and the receivers already have a productive game behind them. Braden Pegan caught eight passes for 81 yards and a touchdown; Larry Simmons added six catches for 110.

The bye’s clearest priority is on the other side of the ball. Utah State scored on a 79-yard run and a 59-yard pass. Two plays produced 138 of the Aggies’ 299 yards. The Utes controlled enough possessions to win comfortably, but those breakdowns made the afternoon less settled than the final margin suggests.

There is room to improve on third down, too: Utah converted three of eight chances in the opener. Better early-down execution would make it easier to keep the offense moving when the next opponent puts a possession in doubt.

The next test is **at Boise State on September 12, with kickoff scheduled for 5 p.m. Eastern**. This week offers a chance to arrive there with the same offensive balance and fewer defensive mistakes. One sharp opener is a useful beginning; the trip to Boise will test whether Utah can reproduce it away from home.

## The rivalry gets a second set of numbers

Utah’s first result set a reasonable standard: productive quarterback play, a back over 100 yards and a clean turnover column. BYU’s opener will give the rivalry its first two-team comparison, though different opponents make any Week 1 verdict provisional.

The Holy War is still waiting in Week 10. For now, the question is smaller and more interesting: what does each team do well enough to trust next Saturday?
