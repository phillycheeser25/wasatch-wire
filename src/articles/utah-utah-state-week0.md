SALT LAKE CITY — The first warning came before Utah had settled in. Javen Jacobs broke a 79-yard touchdown run, Utah State had the lead, and Steve Smith Jr.’s opening afternoon suddenly had a little more edge than the home crowd might have wanted.

The answer took the rest of the game to finish, but it was convincing enough: **No. 19 Utah 36, Utah State 20**. The Utes won the third quarter 13–0, finished with 474 yards and left the opener with a quarterback who had missed only four throws all day.

Devon Dampier completed 27 of 31 passes for 291 yards, one touchdown and no interceptions. He added 53 yards and another score on the ground. It was the kind of performance that lets an offense keep moving without demanding a spectacular play on every possession.

For Smith, the former Utah receiver now leading the Utes, the first result gives his team something concrete to build on. It also leaves him with enough tape to keep the first win from becoming a victory lap.

## Four incompletions. Plenty of answers.

Dampier spread his completions across six receivers. Braden Pegan was the most frequent destination, catching eight passes for 81 yards and the Utes’ only passing touchdown. Larry Simmons supplied the bigger chunks: six catches, 110 yards and a long reception of 47.

Pegan’s four-yard touchdown catch at 4:36 of the third quarter put Utah up 33–14. That came after Dampier’s 12-yard scoring run earlier in the period, when a failed two-point attempt had left the score at 26–14. Two different ways to finish, and the quarter that finally separated the teams.

The passing line deserves attention. So does what never appeared in it: an interception. Utah played the entire game without a turnover while the Aggies committed one.

## Parker gives the afternoon its weight

Wayshawn Parker carried 19 times for 114 yards, scored on a one-yard run late in the first quarter and added five catches for 23 yards. That is 137 yards from scrimmage from a back who gave Dampier a reliable partner rather than asking him to be the whole offense.

Kyri Shoels added a five-yard rushing touchdown late in the second quarter. Utah finished with 183 rushing yards and three scores on the ground, enough balance to keep the opener from turning into a passing exhibition.

Dillon Curtis supplied the margins: field goals from 22, 35 and 36 yards. His kick as the first half expired sent the Utes into the break ahead 20–14. His fourth-quarter field goal made it 36–14 before the Aggies’ final touchdown.

## The part that needs work

Utah State did not have to grind out every yard. Jacobs’ opening touchdown covered 79 yards, and McCae Hillstead found L.J. Johnson Jr. for a 59-yard score with 41 seconds remaining in the first half. Those two plays account for 138 of the Aggies’ 299 yards.

That is the uneasy part of an otherwise controlled result. Utah had 28 first downs to Utah State’s 12 and ran 62 plays to the Aggies’ 35. Yet the visitors averaged 8.5 yards per play. The Utes won the afternoon by sustaining more offense, not by eliminating every explosive threat.

There were smaller inefficiencies, too. Utah converted three of eight third downs and one of two fourth downs. A Big 12 contender will want a cleaner version when the margin for error narrows.

## One win, then a pause

Utah is **1–0 overall and 0–0 in the Big 12**. Week 1 is a bye; the next game is at Boise State in Week 2. Across the mountains, No. 12 BYU is still 0–0 and preparing for Wyoming in Week 1.

One team has played. The other is waiting. The rivalry’s first comparison can wait until both have something on the board.

For now, Utah has the first Saturday: a win, a sharp quarterback and a reminder that even a 16-point margin can leave a coach with work to do.
