FRISCO, Texas — Big 12 Media Days is theater, and everyone knows their role. Coaches say camp will sort it out. Players say they're focused on Week 1. And somewhere between the podiums and the preseason ballots, the league quietly tells you what it actually believes.

Here's what it believes in July 2026: Utah has the best quarterback in the conference, and BYU has the second-best team.

Both fan bases will find something to be furious about in that sentence. That's what makes it a good sentence.

## Dampier, crowned and unsatisfied

Devon Dampier walked out of Frisco as the preseason All-Big 12 quarterback, and the case wasn't complicated. As a junior he threw for 2,490 yards and 24 touchdowns against just five interceptions, ran for 835 yards and ten more scores — and did most of it, we learned this week, while managing a hernia that never let him get fully right.

"I have high expectations for myself," Dampier said, in the tone of a man who considers a preseason honor roughly as filling as a rice cake. "I still have work to do."

The work this year includes a new play-caller. Jason Beck, the offensive coordinator who followed Dampier's career from the start, left for Michigan in the offseason. Kevin McGiven now holds the pen. The scheme bones stay the same — spread, tempo, quarterback-run game as a feature rather than a bug — but a senior quarterback learning a new coordinator's rhythms in August is the kind of detail that decides one-score games in November.

For Morgan Scalley, entering year one with a preseason All-Big 12 quarterback is a gift no first-year head coach should expect. It is also a clock. Dampier is a senior. The window is this season.

## BYU: from hunter to hunted

Down the hall, BYU held court like a program that has run out of ways to sneak up on people. The Cougars have gone 23-4 over the past two seasons and been left standing outside the College Football Playoff velvet rope both times — close enough to hear the music, never let in the door.

Nobody in Frisco was picking them to surprise anyone. The consensus projection slots BYU second in the league, behind only defending champion Texas Tech, and the Cougars put six players on the preseason All-Big 12 team — the most of any program — led by running back LJ Martin.

Kalani Sitake, now the dean of Big 12 coaches in his eleventh year, spent two days deflecting playoff questions with the practiced ease of a man who has answered them since March. The faces of the program did the rest of the talking: Martin, the senior workhorse, and Bear Bachmeier, the sophomore quarterback who arrived in Provo last summer as a true freshman in a three-way quarterback derby and left the season as Big 12 Offensive Newcomer of the Year.

One slight did land in Provo's ledger, and you can be sure the coaching staff wrote it down: the preseason All-Big 12 quarterback nod went to Dampier, not Bachmeier — and Arizona's Noah Fifita took most of the remaining oxygen in that conversation. Fifita brings the Wildcats to LaVell Edwards Stadium on September 12. Circle it.

## What it means for the Holy War

Strip away the podium quotes and the ballots and you get a clean collision course. Utah has the league's most decorated returning quarterback and a first-year head coach with everything to prove. BYU has the league's deepest preseason roster and a two-year résumé the committee keeps refusing to notarize.

One of these teams is going to leave the Holy War with its argument intact. The other is going to spend the rest of the season explaining Frisco's ballots to its own fans.

Camp opens in three weeks. The talking is almost done.
