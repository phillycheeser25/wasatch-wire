SALT LAKE CITY — Some quarterbacks make the defense choose before the snap. Others make it choose again after the play has started. Devon Dampier gives Utah a chance to do both.

That is our read of the player, and the first dynasty box score gives it an encouraging opening: 291 passing yards, 53 rushing yards and two total touchdowns against Utah State. The passing was precise enough that the running never had to rescue the whole afternoon.

## Phoenix, then a different route

Dampier is from Phoenix and attended Saguaro High School. A three-star recruit, he was named Arizona’s MaxPreps Player of the Year in 2022. His senior high-school season included 2,300 passing yards and 1,300 rushing yards.

He began his college career at New Mexico before transferring to Utah. He studies economics. The biography supplies the route; this season will supply the results that belong to Wasatch Wire’s dynasty.

The route is useful because it explains why there is more than one way to think about his position. The running ability is not a late addition to a passing résumé. Both sides of the game were there well before this Utah opener.

## A second answer is not a substitute for the first

Dampier completed 27 of 31 passes in Week 0. That is the number worth returning to when the highlight is his 12-yard rushing touchdown. An offense becomes harder to defend when the quarterback can punish a coverage without first needing it to break down.

Braden Pegan caught eight passes. Larry Simmons finished with 110 receiving yards. Wayshawn Parker ran for 114. Utah’s first win worked because Dampier had productive teammates and used them, not because he had to carry every snap by himself.

Under Steve Smith Jr., that is an attractive starting point. The next step is proving it travels. A Week 1 bye comes first, then a Week 2 visit to Boise State.

## Attention now, an argument later

Dampier joins Martin and Bear Bachmeier on our local Heisman radar. Utah’s opener earns him a first entry, not a place in the national game-generated order.

The real test of a quarterback’s second answer is what happens when the opponent has prepared for the first. This dynasty has a long schedule left to ask that question.
