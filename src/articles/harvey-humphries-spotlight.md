AMERICAN FORK — Harvey Humphries owns a shoebox full of photographs of empty football fields. No touchdowns, no celebrations. Just hash marks, goalposts and the patch of grass where a receiver tried to get away from him.

He takes them on a battered film camera he rebuilt with his grandfather. Developing the pictures takes longer than scrolling through a phone. Humphries likes the wait. A cornerback spends enough time reacting; this is something he gets to slow down.

The **four-star prospect** is **No. 35 nationally, Utah’s No. 1 recruit and the No. 3 cornerback** in the class. At 6-foot-1 and 197 pounds, he has the size to make a receiver work for space. His recruitment has a familiar shape: **Utah first, BYU second**, followed by Oregon, Illinois and Minnesota. He remains uncommitted.

The Humphries family’s football loyalties are less orderly. His mother’s side wears blue. His father’s side wears red. At Thanksgiving, he sits at the end of the table and lets everyone else make the sales pitch. His younger sister keeps a tally of how many times somebody says “tradition.”

Harvey’s own ritual is stranger. Before games, he photographs the opposing sideline, then puts the camera away. Afterward, he writes one sentence on the envelope holding the developed film: what the best receiver taught him. Some envelopes say “don’t open your hips early.” One simply says “he was faster than you thought.”

Both Utah and BYU are pursuing him heavily, and proximity to home matters in his decision. That makes this more than a contest over a highly ranked corner. It is a chance to keep a premier local prospect within reach of the people who have watched him grow.

Utah has the advantage for now. BYU has a short drive and a compelling argument. Humphries has time, a camera and no intention of letting the dinner table choose his college.
