There are two kinds of preseason hype. There's the kind built on projection — recruiting stars, transfer splashes, a coordinator with a podcast following. And there's the kind built on receipts.

BYU's hype is the second kind. The receipts: 23 wins in two seasons. Two playoff cases argued and denied. Six players on the preseason All-Big 12 team — more than anyone else in the conference, including the defending champs. This is not a program hoping to arrive. It arrived two years ago and has been waiting in the lobby ever since.

## 1. LJ Martin is the best offensive player in the conference

Not the best running back. The best *offensive player*. EA's launch ratings made it official — 92 overall, top back in the Big 12, sixth-best in America, fifth-best player in the league at any position — but the film said it first. Martin is a senior workhorse with 89 speed, 84 strength, and a 97 awareness rating, which is the computer's way of saying he's never once been surprised by a defense.

Every great BYU team has had an identity. This one's identity takes the handoff on first down and dares you to be wrong about your fits for four quarters.

## 2. Bear Bachmeier, year two, is the scariest variable in the league

Twelve months ago, Bachmeier was a true freshman who'd been in Provo for a month, buried in a three-way quarterback derby. He ended the season as Big 12 Offensive Newcomer of the Year. That trajectory — from unknown to award-winner in one season — is now getting its first full offseason as the unquestioned starter, with his brother Tiger at receiver and the league's best back behind him.

The ratings computer gave him an 84 and ranked him seventh among Big 12 quarterbacks. Fine. Every great BYU season of the last decade has been fueled by exactly this flavor of disrespect. Tape it to the wall.

## 3. The defense is quietly the best in Provo in a decade

Faletau Satuala (90) might be BYU's best player, period — a strong safety with a 95 awareness rating who erases mistakes before they metastasize. Evan Johnson (90) is a top-ten corner nationally with 96 speed. Isaiah Glasker (87), Keanu Tanuvasa (87), and Cal transfer Cade Uluave (86) give the front seven three more all-conference-caliber pieces.

That's five defenders rated 86 or better. Utah, for comparison, has two. The narrative says BYU wins with scheme and grit. The roster says BYU wins with dudes now.

## 4. The line that protects the investment

Bruce Mitchell (91) is a top-ten center in the country coming off an All-Big 12 season. Andrew Gentry (84) is the third-best tackle in the conference, finally healthy after playing through a Lisfranc recovery. The interior goes three-deep at 83 overall. Bachmeier's development curve gets to happen in a clean pocket.

## 5. The snub math finally favors them

Here's the cold truth about the playoff era: the committee doesn't reward good seasons, it rewards *inevitable* ones. BYU at 11-2 with a soft skin got snubbed. BYU projected second in the league, with the deepest all-conference roster and marquee dates against Arizona (Sept. 12, LaVell Edwards) and at Rice-Eccles — that schedule offers something the last two seasons didn't: proof opportunities. Win the games everyone's watching and there's no discretion left for the committee to exercise.

## The honest caveat

The road Holy War is the trap in the spreadsheet. Utah has the conference's preseason quarterback, the conference's best offensive line, and a stadium that has ended better Novembers than this one. The Cougars going from hunter to hunted means every opponent's best Saturday now happens against BYU.

But hunted teams with 92-overall running backs and top-ten defenses tend to survive other people's best Saturdays. The window in Provo isn't opening. It's open. Kick the door.
