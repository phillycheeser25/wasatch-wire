Every preseason argument about Utah starts the same way — *new coach, new coordinator, prove it* — and every one of them ignores the same inconvenient pile of evidence. So let's stack the evidence.

## 1. The best player in the conference plays quarterback for Utah

The league's media just named Devon Dampier the preseason All-Big 12 quarterback over the guy in Provo and the guy in Tucson. EA's ratings computer put him at 89 overall with a 92-agility engine. His junior season — 2,490 passing yards, 24 touchdowns, five interceptions, 835 rushing yards, ten more scores — happened while he was managing a hernia.

Read that again. The most efficient dual-threat season in the conference came from a quarterback playing hurt for most of it. Utah's offense begins every snap with the single hardest problem in football: a passer the defense must also gameplan as a runner. Healthy Dampier, in his second full year running this system, is the ceiling-raiser for everything else on this list.

## 2. The offensive line is the best in the league, full stop

Spencer Fano is a 93 overall — the highest-rated player on either Holy War roster and the best offensive lineman in the Big 12. Caleb Lomu, the other tackle, is an 89. Tanoa Togiai and Michael Mokofisi bring a combined 500-plus pounds of senior nastiness inside. Four of the five projected starters are seniors or better.

Championship teams in this league are built from the trenches out. Utah does not have a trench question. Utah *is* the trench question other teams have to answer.

## 3. The skill talent is deeper than advertised

Wayshawn Parker (87 overall, 91 speed) is a legitimate feature back. Behind him, sophomore Daniel Bray brings 94 speed — track-meet stuff. The receiver room goes four deep in the high-70s-to-83 band, led by Ryan Davis and Creed Whittemore, both sub-4.5 profiles built for the new coordinator's spacing game.

No, there's no single 92-overall headliner like the one in Provo. There are simply more ways to hurt you, distributed across more bodies, behind a better line.

## 4. The defense never went anywhere

Lost in the offseason coaching noise: Morgan Scalley built this defense, and now the defensive architect runs the whole program. Tao Johnson (91) patrols the back end. Lander Barton (88) and John Henry Daley (88) headline a front seven that returns starters at every level. Smith Snowden is the best slot corner in the conference. This unit finished top-25 nationally in scoring defense last year and returns its spine.

First-year head coaches usually inherit someone else's culture. Scalley inherited his own.

## 5. The schedule cooperates

BYU comes to Rice-Eccles this year. So do Houston and Arkansas. The hardest road trips are winnable, and the Utes miss a chunk of the league's upper crust in cross-division play. A 10-win regular season requires zero miracles — just chalk plus one November coin flip going red.

## The honest caveat

New head coach, new offensive coordinator, senior quarterback learning a new play-caller in August. If it wobbles, it wobbles early — which is exactly why the Utes scheduled their soft launch before conference play arrives.

But here's the thing about Utah teams that nobody picks: they have a habit of being exactly as good as their offensive line, and this offensive line is the best in the Big 12. The pick here isn't sentimental. It's structural.

Arlington in December. You've been warned.
